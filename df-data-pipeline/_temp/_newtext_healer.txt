[h2 type=bar]Tier List Considerations[/h2]
[pad]
[pad]
When putting together the tier list, we have assigned each specialization a rank on an overall scale from [color=q5]S[/color] to [color=q1]D[/color], which is dependent on their ranking in several different categories, including:

[ul]
[li][b]Healing/Absorption[/b]: This includes the performance of a specialization when either healing or preventing damage, the Tanks and raid group will take.[/li]
[li][b]Mana Efficiency[/b]: This takes into account the efficiency of a specialization when healing and how often they may run into Mana issues.[/li]
[li][b]Cooldowns[/b]: This looks at the cooldowns each specialization has for dealing with periods of high incoming damage or other problematic areas.[/li]
[li][b]Group Utility[/b]: This encompasses the buffs and utility a specialization has that helps the party overall.[/li]
[li][b]Offensive Utility[/b]: This looks at how much damage the spec brings to their raid if the encounter allows for it.[/li]
[/ul]

It is important to note that if your chosen specialization is in a lower tier than expected, [b]all of the healers can clear all of the content offered in WoW: Forever[/b].

This list aims to give a baseline understanding of the power levels of each healer, but it's worth mentioning that the healer balancing is much closer than in previous iterations, and healers will excel on different encounters and environments.

[h2 type=bar toc="Tier List Breakdown"]Wow: Forever Healer Tier List Breakdown and Explanations[/h2]

As we mentioned above, several points were considered when assigning each specialization its overall rank, but the actual usage of certain Healers will differ depending on whether your raid group is farming a raid or progressing it for the first time. 

[ol]
[li]Discipline Priest ([color=q5]S-Tier[/color])[/li]
[li]Holy Priest ([color=q5]S-Tier[/color])[/li]
[li]Restoration Shaman ([color=q4]A-Tier[/color])[/li]
[li]Restoration Druid ([color=q4]A-Tier[/color])[/li]
[li]Holy Paladin ([color=q3]B-Tier[/color])[/li]

[/ol]

Every healing spec is performing well on the beta, but there are some differences in each healer's toolkit that impacts their standing in the rankings. 

[color=priest]Priest[/color] is doing well all around, and by picking up [spell=14524], they can output very viable damage without using any mana or interrupting the 5-second rule when it comes to mana regeneration. They are mana efficient and have strong output; [spell=17] can also come in very clutch, particularly with it no longer preventing rage generation for Warriors and Druids. 

[color=shaman]Restoration Shaman[/color] is a solid healer, and the totems they bring are certainly valuable, but before you get access to [spell=8512], the additional free damage that the other healers bring isn't there. With that said, Shamans can certainly get some good damage out by using [spell=3599] and [spell=8349]. At level 30, they are very mana efficient and allow the group to go at a solid pace without having to stop for mana. You can now also very comfortably go down the Enhancement tree to pick up [spell=1223031] and [spell=30812] (once you get additional talent points through the legacy system), which allows the shaman to easily deal with the open world while being a strong healer, and a healer who can output competitive damage.

The [color=druid]Restoration Druid[/color] sticks out for two primary reasons: First, they have a very rounded toolkit with strong sustained healing and high mana regeneration, but secondly, bringing [spell=782] to the tank is such a valuable buff with the current spell power scaling of the buff. 

[color=paladin]Holy Paladin[/color] bring [spell=8990], which fills a similar role to Thorns. At level 30, their healing throughput and mana regeneration are slightly lower than the other healers, but they bring a lot of buffs and utility to make up for that.
