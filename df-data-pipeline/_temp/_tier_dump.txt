Note: At this level bracket, there is very little variance between the specs (bar a few outliers), so a spec's placement is a representation of the class, not necessarily the individual spec. 

## Tier List Considerations

When putting together the tier list, we have assigned each specialization a rank on an overall scale from S to D, which is dependent on their ranking in a number of different categories, including:


- Offensive Throughput: Simply how much damage the spec contributes to the group.
- Buff Utility: What your spec brings to the group that benefits your fellow teammates.
- Non-Buff Utility: Less tangible utility, such as the ability to off-tank in a dire situation.
- Defensive Utility: This is a smaller consideration, but a dead DPS is a bad DPS. Having a toolkit that allows you to play safe by default will also allow you to have higher uptime on the target.

It is important to note, if your chosen specialization is in a lower tier than expected, all of the DPS can clear all of the content offered in WoW: Forever; it may simply be more challenging than if you were playing a higher-ranked specialization.


- Hunter (S-Tier)
- Mage (S-Tier)

- Shaman (A-Tier)
- Paladin (A-Tier)
- Warrior (A-Tier)
- Rogue (A-Tier)

- Feral Druid (B-Tier)
- Warlock (B-Tier)
- Shadow Priest (B-Tier)

- Balance Druid (C-Tier)



It's worth noting that your mileage will vary greatly from player to player and situation to situation. A clear example of this is the Mage's ability to output by far the highest AOE damage in the game during these early levels, but if your group does not pull more than 2-3 mobs at a time, then the Mage will be greatly outshone. Hunter is incredibly strong in those types of environments, but is less adept at AOE damage. 

Overall, melee DPS performs the best at level 20 outside of the two outlier classes. Casters can still keep up for the most part, but mostly through using high-value damage-over-time effects or AOE cooldowns, and then wanding for the bulk of the dungeon. 

Balance Druid is a bit of an outlier at the bottom, as they don't have any wanding option, but bring a high spell power on the tank to compensate.

## Tier List Considerations


When putting together the tier list, we have assigned each specialization a rank on an overall scale from S to D, which is dependent on their ranking in a number of different categories, including:


- Survivability: This includes the specialization's ability to survive high damage as a Main Tank, such as damage-reduction abilities, cooldowns, overall durability, and potential self-sustain.
- Damage & Threat: This takes into account how much damage a specialization can deal and the amount of threat it can generate while still focusing on staying alive and main-tanking.
- Trash & Adds: This focuses mainly on AoE threat generation and survivability.
- Group Utility: This encompasses the utility a specialization has that helps the party overall.


Note: Each tank will have aspects that they are better or worse at dealing with than other tanks. A high ranking does not automatically mean a tank will be particularly adept in all aspects of the game. But at level 20, the value of AOE threat and damage is a lot higher than it might be at the end-game, which skews the tier list.

Honorary mention: Enhancement Shamans do have tanking support, but don't have a full-fledged tank toolkit. They will be perfectly fine or even good to bring as a dungeon tank, but bringing a Shaman tank to a raid will be ill-advised.
